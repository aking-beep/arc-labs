# Research

**Knowledge that outlives the experiment that produced it.**

Research in Labs is written for one reader: the person who opens an adjacent record in
eighteen months. That constraint rules out literature reviews nobody finishes and rules in
short, dated, opinionated notes.

## Shape of a note

One markdown file. Named `YYYY-MM-topic.md`. Four sections:

```markdown
# Topic

**Date:** 2026-10-20 · **Author:** aking · **Confidence:** medium

## The question
What we wanted to know, and which record or decision it was for.

## What we found
The findings. Sources linked. Say where sources disagree rather than averaging them.

## What it implies for Labs
The part with value. If a finding changes nothing, say so — that is also a result.

## What we still do not know
The honest boundary. This section is what makes the note trustworthy.
```

Dated, because research decays and a reader needs to know how stale a claim is.
Attributed, because the reader will want to ask the author a follow-up.
Confidence-rated, because `low` is a legitimate and useful answer.

## Research is not a stage

There is no `research` stage in the pipeline, on purpose. Reading is not progress, and a
record cannot sit in "researching" while nothing is decided. Research supports a record;
it never substitutes for one.

If reading is the cheapest way to falsify a hypothesis, that reading *is* the experiment —
open the record, timebox the reading, write the note, decide. A week of reading with a
decision at the end is a Labs experiment. A month of reading with no decision is a hobby.
